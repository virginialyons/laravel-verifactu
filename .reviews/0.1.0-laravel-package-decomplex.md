## Potential findings

### DEX-001 — Two retry layers inside the queued jobs

- **Evidence:** Confirmed
- **Recommendation:** Act
- **Surface and location / authority:** Decision 6 (the job's answer list) and Decision 12 (the client's automatic retries, "A queued job's own retries come on top").
- **Current-need evidence:** Automatic client retries are needed for the direct calls (Decision 10, owner's choice of the Stripe model). Jobs already retry through `release()` and backoff.
- **Added burden:** The same answers (`429`, `503`, `409 idempotency_key_in_progress`, connection errors) are classified twice: once in the client and once in `IssueInvoice`. Inside a job, the client's wait (up to 3 × 30 s timeouts plus `Retry-After` sleeps) stacks on the job's own waits.
- **Reachable practical impact:** A slow service makes one attempt take over 90 s. That is longer than a queue worker's default 60 s timeout, so the worker kills the job in the middle of its own retries. It is then retried again by the queue, which is safe because of the key, but hard to reason about and to test.
- **Smallest simpler alternative:** One classification, `VerifactuException::isRetryable()` plus `retryAfter`, kept in one place. Direct calls use the client's automatic retries. Jobs call the client with retries off (`retries: 0`) and on a retryable exception do `release($exception->retryAfter)`. Otherwise they fail and fire their event.
- **Exception / boundary check:** The idempotency key and the same-key rule stay as they are. Only where the waiting happens changes.
- **Required behavior and simplification risk:** Same outcomes. Risk: a job forgets to turn retries off. A test asserts that the job makes one request per attempt.
- **Bounded next step or user question:** Reword Decision 6's answer list as "retryable → release, else fail" and say in Decision 12 that jobs run the client without retries.
- **Acceptance signal:** One retry list in the plan. T4's test shows one HTTP request per job attempt.

### DEX-002 — `InvoiceData::reference()` override

- **Evidence:** Confirmed
- **Recommendation:** Act
- **Surface and location / authority:** Decision 4 (`->reference()`) and Decision 6 (the default reference `{morph class}:{key}`).
- **Current-need evidence:** Neither app needs its own reference format; the brief and the owner's answers never ask for one. The package needs to go from a reference back to its model: batch items are matched by `external_reference` (Decision 7), and so is the `409 external_reference_already_used` recovery.
- **Added burden:** With an override, a reference can't be turned back into a model without a stored column or a lookup callback. Neither is in the plan, so batch matching breaks for an app that uses the option.
- **Reachable practical impact:** It's reachable as soon as an app calls `->reference()`: its batch items match no model and are silently skipped.
- **Smallest simpler alternative:** Delete the option. The reference is always derived from the configured model's key (`invoice:{key}`), so the package can always find the model from it.
- **Exception / boundary check:** No external contract needs custom references. The API allows up to 100 characters, which a derived reference always fits.
- **Required behavior and simplification risk:** No owner-required behavior is lost. An option can be added later if an app ever needs one.
- **Bounded next step or user question:** Remove `->reference()` from Decision 4 and state the derivation once.
- **Acceptance signal:** Decisions 4, 6 and 7 use one rule for the reference.

### DEX-003 — Webhook fallback match by `external_reference`

- **Evidence:** Supported
- **Recommendation:** Act
- **Surface and location / authority:** Decision 8 ("found … by `uuid` (or else by `external_reference`)… A job that crashed after a `201` is healed by the next webhook").
- **Current-need evidence:** The case it heals is already healed in two other places:
    - The crashed `IssueInvoice` is retried with the same key and gets the stored `201` again, within 24 hours (Decision 6).
    - After 24 hours, `409 external_reference_already_used` recovers it.
    - Batch items are stored when the batch is applied (Decision 7).
- **Added burden:** A second lookup path in the webhook handler, and its tests. Unknown-invoice handling also has two branches.
- **Reachable practical impact:** It adds work on every webhook for no new behavior, and keeps a third way of creating the model's link to the invoice.
- **Smallest simpler alternative:** Match by `verifactu_uuid` only. An unknown `uuid` is skipped, as for other apps.
- **Exception / boundary check:** For a batch, an `invoices.updated` can arrive before the batch is applied. Skipping it is harmless, because the apply step stores the invoice and the next webhook or `refresh-pending` updates the status.
- **Required behavior and simplification risk:** The status is still kept current. In the early-webhook batch case it is filled a little later instead of a little earlier.
- **Bounded next step or user question:** Drop the fallback from Decision 8 and T5's checks.
- **Acceptance signal:** One lookup by `uuid` in Decision 8.

### DEX-004 — The package-owned `verifactu_invoice_batches` table

- **Evidence:** Supported
- **Recommendation:** Ask user
- **Surface and location / authority:** Decision 7, Decision 15 (the wizard publishes the batches migration, `refresh-pending` applies batches not yet applied), T6, T9.
- **Current-need evidence:** Batches are required (owner). The table exists only so that a batch whose completion webhook was missed can be found and applied once.
- **Added burden:**
    - A package migration, publishing it and the wizard step.
    - A second job in `refresh-pending`.
    - "Applied once" state, and the tests for each.
- **Reachable practical impact:** It's moderate, and every app gets a table, even one that never sends a batch.
- **Smallest simpler alternative:** No table. After posting, `SendInvoiceBatch` dispatches `ApplyInvoiceBatch($uuid)`. That job reads `GET /invoice-batches/{uuid}`, and `release()`s itself every minute until the batch is completed, then applies the items once. The queued job carries the batch UUID, as it carries the idempotency key. The `invoice_batch.completed` webhook is then unnecessary for batches (answer `200`, ignore).
- **Exception / boundary check:** Both designs survive a missed webhook. The table design also survives a lost queue (for example a Redis wipe); the job design does not, any more than any other queued job does.
- **Required behavior and simplification risk:**
    - Results arrive within about a minute of completion instead of at once.
    - The job design gives up the webhook for batches. The owner asked for status through webhooks, and that still holds for every invoice's status (`invoices.updated`).
- **Bounded next step or user question:** "For a batch, which do you prefer? (a) A small package table, plus the 'batch done' webhook and the hourly check. Results arrive at once, and an app gets one extra table. (b) A job that checks the batch every minute until it's done. No table and no batch webhook, and results arrive within a minute."
- **Acceptance signal:** The owner picks one, and Decision 7, T6 and T9 match it.

## Confirmed proportionate areas

- **Configurable column names (Decision 9) and the wizard's questions:** both known apps already have their own number, status and QR columns.
- **`InvoiceData` builder with F2, F3, R1 and R5:** the owner's invoices use exactly F1, F2, F3, R1 and R5.
- **Integer cents with `BcMath\Number`:** both apps store cents, and floats are forbidden for money.
- **Re-reading `GET /invoices/{uuid}` with `ShouldBeUniqueUntilProcessing`:** this removes the ordering column and the event-time logic that the current guide needs.
- **Route outside the `web` group:** no CSRF exemption to document.
- **Seven copied example fixtures in place of a schema validator dependency:** they test the builder against the API's own examples.
- **`Verifactu::fake()` at the HTTP level:** it lets app tests run the package's real jobs and events. Keep its in-memory state to what its assertions and `decide()` need.
- **The direct fix and cancel with one key per call:** the owner's choice, with Stripe's documented safeguards.
- **The AI workflow in the package:** the owner's explicit request.
